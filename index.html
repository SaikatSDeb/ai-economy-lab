ENDOGENOUS MACROECONOMICS / NEW RESEARCH SCENARIO

Can AI productivity create its own customers?

Higher AI productivity expands potential output. Employment, wages, consumption and investment determine how much of that output is purchased—and whether AI adoption can be financed. GDP and AI demand are jointly determined in this illustrative model.

The circular mechanism

AI adoption → productivity → production capacity → displacement and wage income → consumption, investment and government demand → realised GDP → AI purchasing capacity → AI adoption.

Y = min(Y*, C(W, Y − W) + I(Y*, A) + G(Y*, A)); A = A_ceiling × [AI demand capacity]^feedback

The model solves the within-year output–income and adoption–demand loops iteratively, then compounds annual productivity gains from 2026 to 2036. A low labour share need not cause a recession when investment, capital-funded consumption or fiscal demand is strong.

Research foundation and limitations

Acemoglu (2024) cautions against translating task-level productivity directly into enormous GDP gains. Acemoglu & Restrepo (2018) distinguish displacement, productivity and new-task reinstatement. The IMF (2024) analyses how these channels alter income and inequality.

Not a structural general-equilibrium estimate. Consumption propensities (0.9 labour, 0.5 capital), C/I/G baseline shares (60/25/15), employment utilisation elasticity (0.5), adoption trajectory and all other coefficients are stylised, not calibrated from these papers. Prices, fiscal financing, sectoral detail, trade and household wealth are not modelled.

Employment is a demand channel, not a fixed minimum needed for AI profitability. Firms, governments and capital-income recipients can purchase AI even with lower employment.

REPRODUCIBLE MODEL
01

Model equation

Exogenous mode: World GDP × labour share × knowledge-work share × AI-addressed task share × AI spending rate × Anthropic retained share × blended net margin. Feedback mode: GDP, labour income and adoption are solved jointly.

Annual profit = annual flow × six assumptions
02

Shareholder hurdle

Entry share price × 2036 share count × (1 + required return)10 ÷ terminal P/E. The 2036 share count grows with assumed annual dilution.

Terminal P/E analysis ≠ DCF
03

What it does not model

Competition dynamics, capital availability, detailed compute cash outflows, net debt, cross-border tax, contract timing, risk-adjusted discount rates or interim distributions.

Use for reasoning, not forecasting

How assumptions are classified

Reported: IPO disclosures, IMF GDP, ILO labour share, corporate annual net income. Derived: arithmetic from reported numbers. Illustrative: 2036 GDP growth, task share, monetisation, market capture, margins, P/E, dilution, global corporate profit share, and every coefficient of the new endogenous employment–demand module.

Important limitations and correct interpretation Read before drawing conclusions +

Not a probability model. The scenarios have no assigned likelihoods. Outputs are conditional numerical consequences of stated assumptions. A target return hurdle is not an estimate of fair value.

Wage bill is not AI revenue. The model does not assume that automated tasks disappear or that firms will pay model providers the wages they save. Revenue arises only after an independent spending-rate assumption.

Economic production vs corporate earnings. GDP is annual value added. Global profit share is a user-set thought-experiment parameter; US BEA national-accounts corporate profits are not the global equivalent of listed firms' GAAP net income.

Share base and dilution. Basic post-IPO economic shares approximate 13.087bn. The diluted-equivalent share calculation adds the filing's options (treasury method), RSUs, rollover-note shares, acquisitions, donation-matching obligation and public-benefit issuance: approximately 18.013bn at the midpoint $74 assumption. The count includes conditional or future awards and can change. Share price sliders do not recalculate the contractual financing/treasury-method share count. A separate annual dilution slider approximates subsequent additional issuance. Both are simplified.

Capital commitments matter. Anthropic's ~$900.9bn aggregate future commitments span multiple years; they are not all debt, nor should they be subtracted as an immediate lump sum from equity value. Net margin implicitly assumes the eventual costs of running the business, but does not model each lease or purchase obligation.

Fiscal-year differences. The comparator chart uses FY2025 GAAP/IFRS net income except Nvidia FY2026 (ended January 2026). Some net income includes non-operating investment gains, impairments or one-off taxes. Do not read the bars as sustainable annual operating cash flows. The chart is a scale benchmark, not proof of attainability.
